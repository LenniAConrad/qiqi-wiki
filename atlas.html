01

Places within each biome

Groves, open glades and brushy margins break up forests. Ridges, hollows, outcrops, varied cactus branches and mineral vents give the ground its own character. Sparse, low limestone boulders dot dry atoll shores among palms and coastal plants.

02

Coasts with character

Destination islands can be up to 50% wider and longer, with individual bends, unequal ends, headlands and bays. Six broad shape families describe their outlines. Unequal island regions and varied offsets break up repeated rows and gaps while keeping open sea between shores.

03

Climate that connects

Temperature, moisture and elevation guide neighboring habitats. Cold ground passes through boreal and temperate landscapes before tropical forest becomes eligible. Local temperature thins snow into sheltered patches. Mineral deposits taper through irregular margins, and broad, uneven summits retain their steep flanks.

01

What the percentage means

Each percentage is that biome’s share of 6,504,971 equally spaced surface samples across 11 seeds. Ocean, land, rivers, and shores all count. It measures sampled area, not spawn probability or the number of separate biome patches.

02

A repeatable survey

Each seed is sampled from −55,296 to +55,296 metres on both horizontal axes, at 144-metre intervals. These fixed seeds were also used to audit biome coverage; this is a deterministic reference survey, not a statistically random global estimate. Rare or narrow biomes may be underrepresented.

03

Photographs from the game

Every image is a capture of naturally generated terrain through Voxelgame’s renderer, with no packs enabled and the HUD hidden. Viewpoints are chosen to show landforms and habitat details; a photograph can include neighbouring biomes. Field notes describe the implemented terrain, tree canopies and local vegetation. Existing saves retain their original generation rules; these locations use generation 9.
