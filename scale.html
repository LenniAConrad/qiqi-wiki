01

What Voxelgame’s size means

The 8 October 2026 source snapshot accepts ±2²⁸ metres per axis in saves and matching voxel coordinates on the network. That gives a 536,870.912 km square envelope, or 288.23 billion km². Movement and projectiles still suffer precision loss far from the origin; this is not a measured playable boundary or a fully stored map. Reference: core save.rs, MAX_SAVED_VOXEL, half-metre voxels, revision 78b45ed.

02

Width and area tell different stories

Width mode compares actual mean planetary diameters with square map sides. Area mode redraws every object as an equal-area disk: a planet’s whole surface is 4πr², while a map’s area is side². These disks are not physical planet diameters. Planet sizes use NASA mean radii; giant planets use atmospheric reference surfaces. Minecraft uses the Java border convention of ±29,999,984 one-metre blocks. Bedrock, world height and other dimensions are not included.

03

Natural materials, accurate sizes

All textures are original procedural illustrations inspired by natural materials: solar granulation and sunspots, cratered rock, turbulent cloud bands, polar ice and ocean-covered terrain. Fine mountain ridges, river-like channels, cloud shadows and atmospheric haze add depth. Geography and surface features are invented, including Earth’s continents. Voxelgame uses cool island terrain and turquoise shallows; Minecraft uses warmer, chunkier continental terrain. These are not observation photographs or actual game captures. Colors and patterns identify the objects; only their sizes carry measurement meaning. Rings and decorative halos are omitted to preserve body size.
