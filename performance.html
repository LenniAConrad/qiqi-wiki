30 SEPTEMBER 2026 · STREAMING UPDATE

Less rebuilding. Less waiting.

LOD fades now reuse outgoing GPU meshes, edits coalesce, obsolete jobs cancel, and high flight uses distant ground. In the same 640-metre flight replay, detailed residency fell from 1,777 to 543 chunks and recorded detail builds from 4,023 to 2,393. These are replay observations, not a promised FPS multiplier.

LOD choices follow 16-metre chunks inside shared GPU batches. The optional 512-chunk horizon now reaches 8 km by grouping remote terrain; the default stays 64. The fixed forest probe uses 86% fewer remote vertices after replacing tiny branch graphs with rooted trunks, preserving the crowns.

The remaining wildlife-spawn spike fell from 37.2 to 0.26 ms CPU in the same flight replay. In the settled Intel forest comparison, 512 chunks used 2,408 distant batches versus 3,404 at 256, with 1,892 versus 1,792 MiB of vertex buffers. World-pass GPU time was 38.7 versus 37.3 ms. The larger view remains expensive; these offscreen measurements are not windowed FPS or total GPU memory.

28 SEPTEMBER / IMPLEMENTED FOLLOW-UP

Reuse terrain checks. Remove the repeated stall.

A bounded cache now shares surface and canopy bounds between streaming and pruning. Fully settled forest comparisons measured 53–75% less main-thread CPU time, with the same terrain coverage and graphics settings.

Stationary checks become cache hits

The slowest streaming plan fell from 263 to 0.40 ms in pine and 524 to 0.49 ms in rainforest. Each scene avoided all 3,548 repeated surface-band samples across 240 frames. During boosted flight, sampling fell by 96.7% as only new columns needed work.

Coverage stays intact

The 1,024-column cache retains the full ground, canopy and sea interval. Structure and terrain-profile revisions invalidate it. Normal block edits keep their existing occupancy and mesh updates. The cache is bounded, session-local and absent from saves.

GPU pass time remained about 24.3 ms in pine and 34.5 ms in rainforest. CPU reductions are not FPS increases. These shared-machine measurements used frozen source snapshots differing only in the cache implementation. Eight runs that were still streaming were excluded from the settled comparison. The original 27 September analysis below remains a separate dated baseline.

02 / FROM TRUNK TO CANOPY

What do the trees cost?

Hiding near and distant tree geometry reduced measured GPU time by 10–20% in the three forest comparisons. Most rendering cost remained.

These are net scene differences. Removing trees changes occlusion, exposes ground and changes shadow-map work. World occupancy, collision and shadow-ray occupancy remain. The mixed grove is a dense-forest/bamboo boundary, not a pure oak benchmark.

Construction and drawing are different

Explicit tree queries and geometry construction used 0.4–1.1% of process CPU in the detailed loading/flight traces, and were essentially inactive once settled. Shared chunk scans and lighting remain in common categories, so this is not the total CPU cost of trees.

Six rings, two crown resolutions

Near, Fine, Middle and Far use the full crown grid. Horizon and Remote halve it. A separate 267-tree reference probe produced 111,888 versus 24,412 crown vertices: 78% fewer on the last two rings.

The crown probe uses historical generation 4, excludes trunks, and measures construction rather than GPU frame time.

THE STATIONARY SURPRISE

Terrain is rechecked even when you stay still.

Every 0.35 simulated seconds, the streaming plan visits 293 horizontal chunk columns and samples nine heights per column. Pruning repeats these surface checks. The work continues when the requested chunks are already resident.

The final stationary traces recorded individual plans as high as 266 ms in pine and 412 ms in rainforest of thread CPU. Surface bounds accounted for 54% and 68% of process CPU in those traces.

Other compilation/testing sometimes competed for the machine. These spike durations are not an idle-laptop baseline; repeated work was confirmed by call counts and source inspection.

04 / 27 SEPTEMBER PRIORITIES

Start with repeated work.

  1. Reuse unchanged streaming bounds

    Cache bounded per-column intervals and avoid unchanged plans. Preserve full canopy and sea coverage, with correct invalidation for edits and generator/profile changes.

  2. Separate GPU world and shadow costs

    Investigate vegetation, ground cover, shadow drawing and overdraw individually. Bamboo is expensive in these views, but the whole world-pass cost cannot be attributed to bamboo alone.

  3. Reduce meshing and lighting work during flight

    Focus on the large shared CPU categories. Treat tree crown simplification as a targeted experiment with visual checks; the measurements do not establish a need for another terrain LOD stage.

These are the original recommendations from the 27 September analysis. The streaming-bounds cache is covered by the implemented 28 September follow-up above; the remaining items are recommendations for further investigation.

05 / METHOD & EVIDENCE

Keep the measurements in context.

18 stationary runs, plus movement

The settled tables use 16 profiled runs: two normal runs in five habitats and two tree-hidden runs in three forests. Each warmed for 20 seconds and measured for 8. One still-streaming pine run and one inconclusive unprofiled overhead control are excluded.

All captures were offscreen with distant terrain enabled, clear weather, fixed daylight and no mods. Normal player saves were unchanged.

A shared-machine snapshot

GPU hardware pass timestamps exclude uploads and queue waits. Thread CPU scopes separate execution from blocking, but frequency, heat and contention still matter. Two runs per condition provide no confidence interval.

Later CPU traces use separately preserved instrumented builds. These snapshots are independent of the atlas’s biome-photo build. Normal game builds compile the profiling hooks out.
